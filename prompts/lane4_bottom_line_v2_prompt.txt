BOTTOM LINE RULES

3-4 sentences maximum. Every word earns its spot.
Never reference acceptance rates, admit percentages, SAT ranges, GPA stats, or applicant ratios anywhere in Bottom Line. Zero admissions data. That belongs in the Admissions section only.
Closing line tells the family what to do or what this means. Not "deserves a look" or "deserves attention." Those are dead phrases. Study the exemplar closers: "This one deserves a visit." "This is one of the best decisions he could make." "Don't let this one slip by." "Let the swimming catch up and this one opens up." Short. Direct. Lands.
No m-dashes. No money talk. No consolation-prize language. Dead phrases: "a real option," "a worthwhile target," "deserves a look," "deserves attention," "Your times are competitive." Never use "legitimate" to describe a school. It is fine when describing swim fit.
Beat 1: What makes the school elite, specific and earned.
Beat 2: The swim truth with meat, not raw data. Voice follows the recruiting likelihood label.
Beat 3: The close that lands.

Dead Phrases to Kill (NEVER USE):
- "deserves a real look" ❌
- "deserves attention" ❌
- "a worthwhile target" ❌
- "deserves a visit" (standalone) ❌
- "don't let this one slip by" (standalone) ❌
- "a genuine option" ❌
- "this is a list-topper" (vague) ❌
- "worth pursuing" (passive) ❌

Directives That Land:
- "Apply, engage the coach early, and know exactly where you stand before decision day" ✅
- "Email the coach with your times—this is the kind of fit where they respond fast" ✅
- "Get on the coach's radar fast—this is a fit that moves quickly" ✅
- "Build the rest of the list with the same seriousness" ✅
- "Reach out directly; this is where coaches respond fast" ✅

NO HEDGE LANGUAGE:
- Never say "approximately," "around," "estimated," "likely," "probably," "may"
- State numbers and facts as fact
- If uncertain, don't include it

EXEMPLARS BY RECRUITING LIKELIHOOD

Top Recruit (Case Western, D3 UAA):
One of the country's elite STEM universities, steps from the
Cleveland Clinic and NASA, and the kind of degree that opens
doors before the ink dries. In the pool, Joseph's 1650 is a
UAA weapon. He scores points, he doesn't just fill a lane.
This one deserves a visit.

Conference Star (Johns Hopkins, D3 Centennial):
Johns Hopkins is one of the great research universities in
the world, and the Blue Jays are the program the rest of
the Centennial is chasing. Joseph's 1650 and 500 score
points in this conference, but he should study that roster
before assuming a spot. If the academics are the draw, this
is one of the best decisions he could make.

Conference Star (Macalester, D3 MIAC):
One of the truly great liberal arts colleges in the country,
sitting inside a real city with real energy. Joseph walks in
as a scoring threat who also happens to be exactly the student
Macalester recruits. Don't let this one slip by without a
serious look.

OOU Reach (Stanford, D1):
The best university in the world isn't hyperbole here, it's
the argument a lot of serious people make out loud.
Academically, Joseph belongs in the conversation. In the pool,
Stanford is the deepest roster in college swimming at any
level. The path in is narrow and the competition is
Olympic-level. Apply because it's Stanford and he's earned
the right to try. Build the rest of the list like a serious
recruiter, not a dreamer.

Edge Case (Carnegie Mellon, D3 UAA):
One of the most respected technical universities on the planet,
and a CS or engineering degree here carries weight in every
room it enters. In the pool, Joseph is right on the edge of
what CMU recruits. The fit is real but the margin is thin, and
a time drop before applications close changes the conversation
meaningfully. The academics are a lock. Let the swimming catch
up and this one opens up.
