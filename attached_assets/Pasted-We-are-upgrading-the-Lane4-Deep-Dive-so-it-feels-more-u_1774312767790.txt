We are upgrading the Lane4 Deep Dive so it feels more useful, more specific, and less generic. This is not a rewrite for more words. It is a rewrite for better substance.

Read carefully and follow exactly.

OBJECTIVE

The current deep dive is too general. It sounds polished, but it does not always answer the questions a student or parent actually has.

We want the deep dive to stay readable, but become much more valuable in two areas:

1. Academic Program depth
2. Student Experience relevance

This should be driven by:
- intended major(s)
- the user’s free response profile

IMPORTANT STYLE RULES

1. No em dashes anywhere
- Do not generate em dashes in any deep dive or expanded section
- Use commas, periods, or parentheses instead

2. Do not sound like AI
Avoid:
- generic brochure language
- vague “holistic” phrases
- repetitive sentence structure
- overexplaining obvious things
- padded transitions
- stiff consultant tone

3. Do not overpersonalize
Do NOT write things like:
- “for a student like you”
- “a kid like you who enjoys fishing”
- “because you said you value collaboration”

Instead, use the free response lightly and naturally.

Good:
- “There is good access to fishing nearby.”
- “This environment tends to be more collaborative than cutthroat.”
- “Hands-on learning is a real strength here.”

Bad:
- “Since you like fishing, you will love the nearby fishing.”
- “Because you want collaboration, this is a perfect fit.”

4. Be specific when discussing the major
This is the main place to go deep.
This section should feel informed and concrete, not generic.

CORE PRODUCT CHANGE

We want the Deep Dive to use two forms of personalization:

A. Intended major(s)
B. Free response profile

The major should drive the deepest and most specific content.
The free response should lightly shape what gets emphasized in Student Experience and overall fit language.

PART 1 — MAJOR HANDLING

We need a better way to lock in 1 to 2 possible majors.

Implement:
- Primary intended major
- Secondary intended major (optional)

This should be a structured input, not just buried in free response.

These major selections should drive:
- Academic Program section
- Outcomes / career section
- any expanded “More” content tied to academics

If no major is selected:
- fall back to a lighter academic overview
- do not pretend to know the student’s academic priorities

PART 2 — FREE RESPONSE USAGE

Use the free response carefully and lightly.

Goal:
Extract a small number of meaningful preferences or priorities that can shape emphasis, not facts.

Examples of useful signals:
- collaborative vs highly competitive environment
- hands-on learning
- smaller classes
- urban vs suburban vs college town feel
- outdoors access
- social energy
- pace / intensity

Do NOT:
- overfit tiny details
- force every preference into every school
- make the writing sound like it is talking directly back to the user’s hobbies

If a free response mentions a hobby or lifestyle preference, handle it lightly.
Example:
- mention nearby hiking, fishing, skiing, city access, etc.
- do not build the whole section around it

PART 3 — DEEP DIVE CONTENT CHANGES

A. Academic Program section
This is the highest-priority upgrade.

When a major is selected, this section should become much more detailed and useful.

It should include, where relevant and available:
- exact department or program name
- whether the major is housed in engineering, arts and sciences, or another structure
- how established or respected the program is
- undergraduate research access
- lab opportunities
- internship pathways
- class size experience within the program
- faculty access
- practical vs theoretical tilt
- employer or graduate school outcomes
- how this program is generally viewed by employers or grad schools
- anything school-specific that makes the program distinctive

This should feel like real meat, not generic “strong academics” language.

For high-interest majors like BME, go especially deep.
A student clicking “More” on the major section should get a serious, substantive readout.

B. Student Experience section
This should use the free response lightly.

The goal is not to say “you would love this.”
The goal is to highlight relevant aspects of the environment in a grounded way.

Good examples:
- collaboration vs intensity
- student culture
- whether the school feels more balanced or more all-in academically
- whether social life revolves around campus, city, team, etc.
- nearby outdoor access or city access if relevant

Avoid fluff:
- beautiful campus
- vibrant student life
- strong sense of community
unless there is actual substance behind it

C. Admissions reality
Do NOT create a giant expanded section here.
Do NOT invent detailed strategy.

But in the standard deep dive, include a sharper statement about whether swimming meaningfully moves the needle.

Examples of the kind of thing we want:
- “Swim helps here, but you still need to be strong academically.”
- “Coach support can matter here.”
- “Swim alone is not enough here.”

Keep this concise and grounded.
Do not drift into made-up ED advice or fake certainty.

D. Swim section
Do NOT add a new deep “More” section for swim.
We do not have enough grounded information to safely expand that section without speculating.

PART 4 — EXPANDABLE “MORE” SECTIONS

Add “More” support only where we can provide real value safely.

Priority:
1. Academic Program / Major
2. Outcomes / career direction
3. Student Experience

Do NOT add “More” for swim.
Do NOT create fluffy “More” sections that just repeat the same content in longer form.

PART 5 — WRITING QUALITY RULES

The writing must:
- sound natural
- sound specific
- sound informed
- not sound AI-generated
- not overpersonalize
- not overclaim
- not read like marketing copy

Avoid:
- “This school offers a wide range of opportunities”
- “Students can take advantage of”
- “vibrant”
- “dynamic”
- “renowned”
- “world-class” unless clearly warranted
- repetitive “this is a place where...”

Prefer:
- direct statements
- concrete examples
- honest tradeoffs
- plain English

PART 6 — IMPLEMENTATION GOALS

Update the deep dive generation so that:
- major drives the most detailed academic content
- free response lightly influences emphasis
- the output remains readable and not bloated
- expanded sections contain real substance
- no em dashes appear anywhere
